The Challenges

Why Personalization Stalls Before It Pays Off

Most banks are not short on ambition or pilots. They are short on production-grade infrastructure and proof. Four patterns show up again and again.

{{ICON:puzzle}}

Stuck at pilot stage

Working pilots and bold targets, but revenue impact stays forward-looking, unattributed or undisclosed years into the initiative.

{{ICON:data-quality}}

Fragmented customer data

41% of brands name siloed, non-centralized customer data as personalization's primary blocker, leaving models with nothing reliable to score against.

{{ICON:integrations}}

Models disconnected from delivery

A trained model sitting in a lab does not move revenue. It has to be wired into live channel and pricing decisions to matter.

{{ICON:chart}}

No link between spend and result

Without built-in attribution, a bank cannot separate the model's uplift from the market's, so the initiative cannot defend its own budget.

FAQ

The Questions We Get First

How is this different from the propensity models we have already invested in?

Propensity scoring is the legacy layer, not the differentiator. BCG frames it as being superseded by uplift modelling, which targets the customers who convert because of an intervention rather than those who would have converted anyway. Your existing propensity work is a starting point, not the destination.

Do we need a single customer view before we can start?

Not to start, but to scale. 41% of brands name fragmented customer data as personalization's primary blocker, so it has to be solved. But we typically sequence unification alongside early decisioning work rather than treating it as a year-long prerequisite.

Is this generative AI or classical machine learning?

Both, depending on the capability. Real-time decisioning and behavioural segmentation lean on modern ML and AI techniques; lifetime-value modelling is often solid classical ML. We are explicit about which is which rather than labelling every component "AI".
